To get a copy of a birth certificate in Barbados, you can follow these steps:

1. **Eligibility**: You can request a copy for anyone whose birth was registered in Barbados. If you are requesting it for someone else, you must disclose your relationship to them and the reason for the request.

2. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

3. **Application Process**: There are two ways to apply:
   - **Online**: You need a debit or credit card and an EZPay+ account. Complete the application form online. Note that you cannot save your progress, so complete it in one go.
   - **In-person**: You can also apply in person at the Applications Desk at the Registration Department.

4. **Information Required**: You will need to provide:
   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.
   - The full names of the parents of the person named on the certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it can sometimes be issued on the same day with proof of the emergency.

6. **Contact Information**: For further assistance, you can contact the Registration Department:
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb

## 32. What does the Ministry of Labour do?
*government-services · answered · 7.8s*

The Ministry of Labour in Barbados has several key responsibilities:
- **Promoting Decent Work**: It aims to create opportunities for decent and productive work while ensuring conditions of freedom of association, equity, security, and human dignity.
- **Workforce Development**: The Ministry works to develop a well-informed and properly trained Barbadian workforce, including individuals with disabilities, to meet local and regional labor market needs.
- **Industrial Relations**: It fosters harmonious industrial relations and cordial conciliation to support quality employment conditions.
- **Legislation and Policy**: The Ministry formulates policies and implements legislation to promote rights at work and enhance employment conditions.
- **Child Labour Elimination**: It actively works to eliminate all forms of child labour in Barbados.

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 5.1s*

The history of sugar in Barbados dates back to the 1640s when the island transitioned from a small farm economy to large plantations primarily focused on sugar production. This shift was significantly influenced by the introduction of enslaved African labor, which replaced the earlier indentured European workers. 

By the mid-17th century, Barbados had become a leading sugar producer, with over 500 windmills established for sugar processing, making it one of the highest densities of such structures in the world, second only to the Netherlands. The sugar industry not only transformed the island's economy but also played a crucial role in its social and cultural development.

Throughout the 18th and 19th centuries, sugar production continued to thrive, contributing to the wealth of Barbados and Britain. However, by the early 20th century, the industry began to decline as tourism became the dominant economic force on the island. Today, the legacy of sugar is preserved through various museums and cultural events, such as the Barbados Sugar and Rum Season, which celebrates this rich heritage.

## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 6.5s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an African-born slave, who organized the uprising in response to the intolerable conditions faced by enslaved people under the Barbados Slave Code. This code denied slaves basic human rights and allowed for brutal punishments.

The rebellion was fueled by a growing sentiment among the enslaved population that their treatment was unacceptable, and many believed that the political climate in Britain might allow for negotiations for freedom. Bussa became a prominent figure in the rebellion, which was initially surprising to the planters, who thought their slaves were content due to certain allowances like weekly dances.

However, the revolt was ultimately suppressed by British forces, resulting in significant loss of life among the rebels. The harsh response to the uprising shocked many in England and contributed to the growing abolitionist movement, which eventually led to the abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833. Bussa's legacy is commemorated by the Bussa Emancipation Statue, which symbolizes the "breaking of chains" of slavery in Barbados.

## 78. What is Harrison's Cave?
*visitors · answered · 5.3s*

Harrison's Cave is a popular tourist attraction located in the central uplands of Barbados. It is known for its stunning stalactite and stalagmite formations, which have been created over thousands of years through water erosion of limestone rock. Visitors can explore the cave via a tram tour that takes them through various sections, including a large cavern known as "The Great Hall." The cave also features underground streams and waterfalls, providing a unique natural experience.

The cave was first mentioned in historical documents in 1795, but serious exploration did not occur until the 1970s. It has since been developed into a major tourist destination, offering guided tours that allow visitors to appreciate its geological beauty and learn about its history. Harrison's Cave is considered one of the "Seven Wonders of Barbados."

## 91. What exactly are Bajan fish cakes, and how are they prepared?
*client · answered · 6.8s*

Bajan fish cakes are a traditional Barbadian dish made primarily from salted cod, flour, and various seasonings. They are typically enjoyed as a snack or breakfast item, often served hot and sometimes accompanied by sauces like Marie Rose or pepper sauce.

### Preparation:
1. **Prepare the Salted Cod:**  
   - Boil 1 lb of boneless, skinless salt cod in 6 cups of water for about 4 minutes. Discard the water and boil it again in fresh water for another 3 minutes.  
   - Flake the fish with a fork after boiling.

2. **Mix Ingredients:**  
   - In a bowl, combine the flaked fish with 1 cup of flour, 1 tsp baking powder, 1 diced onion, 1 small finely diced hot pepper, and chopped herbs like parsley, thyme, and marjoram.  
   - Add 1 lightly beaten egg and about 1 cup of water to form a thick batter. Adjust the consistency with more water or flour as needed.

3. **Fry the Fish Cakes:**  
   - Heat oil in a pan over medium heat. Drop teaspoonfuls of the batter into the hot oil, being careful not to overcrowd the pan.  
   - Fry until golden brown, turning as necessary to ensure even cooking. Drain on paper towels.

4. **Serve:**  
   - Enjoy the fish cakes hot, either on their own or in a sandwich with salt bread, known locally as "bread and two."

Sources: [Barbados Recipes: Fish Cakes](https://barbados.org/barbados-recipes-fishcakes.htm) · [Fish Cakes and Sauce Marie Rose Recipe - Totally Barbados](https://www.totallybarbados.com/articles/barbados-recipes/fish-cakes-sauce-marie-rose-recipe) · [Explore Barbados Street Food](https://www.visitbarbados.org/barbados-street-food)

## 92. What is the difference between pudding and souse and cou-cou and flying fish?
*client · answered · 13.6s*

- **Pudding and Souse**: This is a traditional Barbadian dish that consists of pickled pork (souse) served with a side of sweet potato pudding. The souse is typically made by marinating the pork in a mixture of vinegar, lime juice, and spices, while the pudding is a sweet, dense dish made from grated sweet potatoes, sugar, and spices, often baked until firm.

- **Cou-Cou and Flying Fish**: Cou-cou is another traditional Barbadian dish made from cornmeal and okra, which is cooked until it reaches a smooth, thick consistency. It is often served as a side dish. Flying fish is a type of fish that is a national symbol of Barbados and is commonly served alongside cou-cou, making it a popular meal known as "cou-cou and flying fish." The fish is typically seasoned and fried or steamed.

In summary, pudding and souse is a meat and pudding dish, while cou-cou and flying fish is a combination of a starch (cou-cou) and a fish (flying fish).

## 99. Can I legally operate a food vending business from a vehicle in Barbados, and which approvals are required?
*client · answered · 10.3s*

Yes, you can legally operate a food vending business from a vehicle in Barbados, but you will need to obtain the appropriate licenses. Here are the approvals required:

- **Food Business Licence**: This is necessary if you handle food in any capacity, including selling food from a vehicle. You must apply for this licence before starting your business. The application will be reviewed by Environmental Health, and an inspection of your food business will be conducted before the licence is issued.

- **Temporary Restaurant Licence**: If your food vending setup is considered a temporary restaurant (operating for up to 30 days), you may need to comply with specific regulations regarding food safety and handling.

Make sure to adhere to the Health Services (Food Hygiene) Regulations, 1969, which outline the legal requirements for handling food safely. 

For more detailed information on the application process, you may refer to the official government sources or visit your local polyclinic for assistance.

Sources: [Apply for a food business licence](https://alpha.gov.bb/business-trade/apply-for-food-business-licence) · [Temporary restaurants: what you need to know](https://alpha.gov.bb/business-trade/temporary-restaurants-what-you-need-to-know) · [Apply for a restaurant licence](https://alpha.gov.bb/business-trade/apply-for-restaurant-licence)
