## 14. How do I register a business in Barbados?
*resident-top-20 · answered · 5.5s*

To register a business in Barbados, follow these steps:

1. **Determine the Type of Business**: Decide whether you are registering as an individual, firm, limited partnership, or external company.

2. **Complete the Application**: 
   - For individual or firm registration, complete Form 1, which is the application for registration of a business name.
   - For limited partnerships, use the specific registration form for limited partnerships.
   - For external companies, complete Form 28.

3. **Submit the Application**: You can submit your application online through the Business Barbados portal or book an appointment at their office.

4. **Pay the Filing Fee**: 
   - The fee for registering a business name is BDS $104.00.
   - The fee for registering a limited partnership is BDS $2,400.00.

5. **Review Process**: After submission, Business Barbados Officers will review your application. If everything is in order, your business will be registered under the approved name.

6. **Registration Timeline**: Registration is open throughout the year, and you must register within fourteen (14) days after commencing business if you are using a business name that does not consist of your true surname.

For more detailed information, you can visit the [Business Barbados website](https://caipo.gov.bb/corporate-affairs/business-name-registration).

## 21. How do I get a birth certificate in Barbados?
*government-services · answered · 7.7s*

To get a copy of a birth certificate in Barbados, you can follow these steps:

1. **Eligibility**: You can request a copy for anyone whose birth was registered in Barbados. If you are requesting on someone else's behalf, you must disclose your relationship to them and the reason for the request.

2. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

3. **Application Process**: There are two ways to apply:
   - **Online**: You will need a debit or credit card and an EZPay+ account. Complete the application in one go, as it cannot be saved for later.
   - **In Person**: You can also complete a paper form at the Applications Desk.

4. **Required Information**: You will need to provide:
   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.
   - The full names of the parents of the person named on the certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it may be issued on the same day with proof of the emergency.

6. **Contact Information**: For further assistance, you can contact the Registration Department:
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700 or +1 (246) 535-9751 (if calling from overseas).

For more details, you can visit the official government page on [getting a birth certificate](https://alpha.gov.bb/family-birth-relationships/get-birth-certificate).

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 7.1s*

The history of sugar in Barbados dates back to the 1640s when the cultivation of sugar cane began to transform the island's economy. Initially, Barbados was an agricultural economy focused on small-scale farming, but this changed dramatically with the introduction of sugar production. The island became a major player in the sugar industry, leading to the establishment of large plantations worked by enslaved Africans.

- **Early Development**: The exiled Jewish community introduced windmill technology for sugar production, leading to the construction of over 500 windmills in Barbados, making it the second highest in density globally, after the Netherlands.
- **Economic Transformation**: By the late 17th century, sugar had become the dominant economic force, overshadowing other crops like cotton and tobacco. The wealth generated from sugar production significantly impacted both local and British economies.
- **Cultural Significance**: Sugar and rum are deeply intertwined with Barbadian culture and history, shaping social structures and contributing to the island's identity.

The legacy of sugar production is preserved in places like the Sir Frank Hutson Sugar Museum, which showcases the history and machinery used in sugar production during the 18th and 19th centuries. The museum is located at the Portvale Sugar Factory, where visitors can learn about the evolution of sugar production in Barbados.

## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 5.1s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an African-born slave, who organized the uprising in response to the intolerable conditions faced by enslaved people under the Barbados Slave Code. This code denied slaves basic human rights and allowed for brutal punishments.

The rebellion was fueled by a growing sentiment among the enslaved population that they deserved freedom, especially as political changes in Britain suggested that negotiations for emancipation might be possible. Bussa and other leaders, including the literate freedman Nanny Grigg, aimed to challenge the oppressive system.

However, the revolt was poorly timed and ultimately failed. The British colonial authorities swiftly suppressed the uprising, resulting in significant loss of life among the rebels. The aftermath of the rebellion shocked many in Britain and contributed to the growing abolitionist movement, which eventually led to the abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833.

## 80. What should I know about driving in Barbados?

- **Driving License**: You need a valid driving license from your home country to drive in Barbados. An international driving license is not required.

  - $10.00 for less than two months
  - $100.00 for two months and up to a maximum of one year.

- **Speed Limits**: Speed limits are 80 km/h (50 mph) on major highways, 60 km/h (37 mph) in other areas, and 40 km/h (25 mph) in city areas.
- **Cellphone Use**: Only hands-free conversations via Bluetooth are legal; using a cellphone while driving is prohibited.
- **Public Transport**: Besides driving, you can use public transport options like the Barbados Transport Board, Public Service Vehicles (PSVs), Route Taxis/ZR, and Taxi Cabs for getting around the island.

## 84. What should I do to prepare for a hurricane?
*everyday-life · answered · 6.6s*

To prepare for a hurricane in Barbados, follow these steps:

- **Secure Your Home**:
  - Install shutters or board up windows.
  - Ensure your roof and foundation are secure.
  - Trim tree branches that could fall.

- **Emergency Supplies**:
  - Store at least 3 gallons of drinking water per person for 3 days.
  - Have enough non-perishable food (canned goods, dry crackers) for at least 3 days.
  - Prepare a portable first-aid kit and keep essential medications on hand.
  - Keep important documents (ID, insurance papers, birth certificates) in a waterproof bag.
  - Have cash in small bills available, as ATMs may not work after the storm.

- **Communication**:
  - Charge all devices and power banks.
  - Keep a battery-powered or wind-up radio to receive updates from the Department of Emergency Management (DEM).
  - Write down important phone numbers and emergency contacts.

- **Plan for Evacuation**:
  - Know the location of the nearest emergency shelter and have a plan for your family.
  - Move household items to higher levels if you live in low-lying areas.

- **Stay Informed**:
  - Monitor local radio stations for updates and advisories.
  - Follow any official notifications regarding shelter openings and safety measures.

- **Before the Storm Hits**:
  - Bring in outdoor furniture and loose items.
  - Fill your vehicle with gas.
  - Turn off all electrical appliances and remove TV antennas from the roof.

For a detailed checklist, you can refer to the [StormReady Barbados checklist](https://alpha.gov.bb/health-and-emergency-services/stormready/checklist).

## 91. What exactly are Bajan fish cakes, and how are they prepared?
*client · answered · 6.2s*

Bajan fish cakes are a traditional dish in Barbados, made primarily from salted codfish mixed with flour, spices, and seasonings. They are typically deep-fried until golden brown and are often enjoyed hot, sometimes served with sauces like Marie Rose or pepper sauce.

### Preparation Steps:
1. **Prepare the Salted Cod:**  
   - Boil the salted cod in water for about 4 minutes, discard the water, then boil again for another 3 minutes. This helps to reduce the saltiness. 
   - Flake the fish with a fork after boiling.

2. **Mix Ingredients:**  
   - In a bowl, combine the flaked fish with diced onion, flour, baking powder, a lightly beaten egg, finely diced hot pepper, and chopped herbs (like parsley, thyme, and marjoram).
   - Add water gradually until a thick batter forms.

3. **Fry the Fish Cakes:**  
   - Heat oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Drop spoonfuls of the batter into the hot oil, being careful not to overcrowd the pan.
   - Fry until golden brown, turning as necessary to ensure even cooking.
   - Drain on paper towels before serving.

Bajan fish cakes are often enjoyed as a snack or part of a meal and can be served on their own or in a sandwich known as "bread and two" when placed in salt bread.
