## 21. How do I get a birth certificate in Barbados?
*government-services · answered · 6.3s*

To get a copy of a birth certificate in Barbados, you can follow these steps:

1. **Eligibility**: You can request a copy for anyone whose birth was registered in Barbados. If you are requesting on someone else's behalf, you must disclose your relationship to them and the reason for the request.

2. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

3. **Application Methods**:
   - **Online**: You will need a debit or credit card and an EZPay+ account to complete the application online. Note that you cannot use the online service if you want the certificate sent overseas.
   - **In-Person**: You can complete a paper application form and submit it at the Applications Desk. You will receive a receipt upon submission.

4. **Information Required**: The application form will ask for:
   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.
   - The full names of the parent(s) of the person named on the certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it can sometimes be issued on the same day if proof of the emergency is provided.

6. **Contact Information**: For more details or if you live overseas, you can contact the Registration Department at:
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700 or +1 (246) 535-9751 (if calling from overseas).

For more information, you can visit the official government page: [Get a copy of a birth certificate](https://alpha.gov.bb/family-birth-relationships/get-birth-certificate).

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 5.1s*

The history of sugar in Barbados dates back to the early 17th century. Initially, after the English settled in 1625, the island's economy was based on small-scale farming of tobacco and cotton. However, by the 1640s, the introduction of sugar cane transformed the agricultural landscape. This shift was driven by the need for a more profitable cash crop, as tobacco and cotton yielded poor economic returns.

- **Sugar Revolution**: The transition to sugar production marked a significant change, leading to the establishment of large plantations worked by enslaved Africans. This shift resulted in the consolidation of land holdings and the decline of small farmers.
- **Economic Impact**: Sugar became the dominant crop, and by the 18th century, Barbados was known for its high density of sugar windmills, second only to the Netherlands. The cultivation of sugar cane and the associated rum production became central to the Barbadian economy.
- **Cultural Significance**: The sugar industry not only shaped the economy but also influenced the social and cultural fabric of Barbados, intertwining with the island's identity.

The legacy of sugar continues to be celebrated in Barbados, particularly through events like the Sugar and Rum Season, which highlights the historical significance of these industries in the island's development.

## 54. What is the Barbados Garrison?

The Barbados Garrison is a historic military complex that was the largest in the British Colonies during the 18th and 19th centuries. It began with the construction of St. Ann's Fort in 1705 and expanded to include soldiers' barracks, a parade ground, and a commissariat. ## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 5.4s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an African-born slave, and was fueled by the intolerable conditions faced by enslaved people in Barbados. Despite the planters' belief that their slaves were content due to allowances for social activities, the harsh realities of the Barbados Slave Code, which denied slaves basic human rights, contributed to the uprising.

The rebellion was characterized by a growing sentiment among the enslaved population that their treatment was unacceptable, and many believed that the political climate in Britain was favorable for negotiating freedom. Bussa emerged as a prominent figure among the organizers, which included both enslaved individuals of higher status and literate freedmen. However, the rebellion was ultimately suppressed by British forces, resulting in significant loss of life among the rebels. The uprising was triggered prematurely, and the enslaved individuals were outmatched by the colonial authorities.

The aftermath of the rebellion shocked the British public and contributed to the growing abolitionist movement, leading to the eventual abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833. Bussa's legacy is commemorated in Barbados, notably through the Bussa Emancipation Statue, which symbolizes the struggle for freedom and the breaking of chains of slavery.

## 58. What is tuk band music?
*history-culture · answered · 4.2s*

Tuk band music, also known as rukatuk, is a traditional Barbadian musical genre that emerged during the colonial era. It combines elements of African-based rhythms with European military music, reflecting the island's cultural history. The music is typically performed by a tuk band, which consists of instruments such as a double-headed bass drum, triangle, flute, snare drum, and the pennywhistle, which has replaced the traditional fiddle in modern performances.

The genre is symbolic of revolution in Barbadian cultural music and history, having developed when traditional Afro-based drums were banned. Tuk band music is characterized by its vibrant and lively rhythms, making it an integral part of Barbados' cultural heritage and celebrations.

## 91. What exactly are Bajan fish cakes, and how are they prepared?
*client · answered · 6.1s*

Bajan fish cakes are a traditional dish in Barbados, typically made from salted codfish mixed with flour, spices, and seasonings. They are often enjoyed as a breakfast item or as a snack, and can be served on their own or in a sandwich known as "bread and two" (fish cakes in salt bread). 

### Ingredients:
- 1 lb boneless, skinless salt cod
- 1 cup flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 small hot pepper, finely diced
- Chopped parsley, thyme, and marjoram
- 1 cup water
- 1 egg, lightly beaten

### Preparation Steps:
1. **Prepare the Salted Cod:** Boil the salt cod in 6 cups of water for 4 minutes, then discard the water. Boil again in fresh water for 3 minutes. Flake the fish with a fork after boiling.
2. **Mix Ingredients:** In a bowl, combine the flaked fish with the flour, baking powder, diced onion, hot pepper, herbs, and the beaten egg. Gradually add water until a thick batter forms.
3. **Fry the Fish Cakes:** Drop spoonfuls of the batter into hot oil over medium heat, being careful not to overcrowd the pan. Fry until golden brown, turning as necessary to ensure even cooking.
4. **Drain and Serve:** Remove the fish cakes from the oil and drain on paper towels. They are best served hot, often with a dipping sauce like Marie Rose sauce or traditional Barbados pepper sauce.

## 92. What is the difference between pudding and souse and cou-cou and flying fish?
*client · answered · 6.0s*

**Pudding and Souse**  
- **Pudding**: Made from grated sweet potato seasoned with onion, thyme, chives, a little sugar, salt, and butter, then cooked (usually baked).  
- **Souse**: Consists of boiled pork (often using the head, tails, and feet) pickled in lime juice, mixed with grated cucumber, sweet peppers, onion, parsley, and scotch bonnet pepper.  
- **Served Together**: Pudding and souse are typically served together, especially on Saturdays in Barbados.

**Cou-Cou and Flying Fish**  
- **Cou-Cou**: A dish made primarily from cornmeal and okra, often served as a side dish. It is a staple in Barbadian cuisine and can be paired with various meats and fish.
- **Flying Fish**: A fish that is a national symbol of Barbados and is often prepared in various ways, including fried or as part of the dish "Cou-Cou and Flying Fish," which is considered the national dish of Barbados.  
- **Tradition**: Cou-Cou is traditionally eaten with flying fish, making it a classic combination in Barbadian meals.
