## 21. How do I get a birth certificate in Barbados?
*government-services · answered · 7.4s*

To get a copy of a birth certificate in Barbados, follow these steps:

1. **Eligibility**: You can request a copy for anyone whose birth was registered in Barbados. Barbadian citizens can request copies at any time.

2. **Application Process**: There are two ways to apply:
   - **Online**: You need a debit or credit card and an EZPay+ account. Complete the application form online.
   - **In Person**: Visit the Registration Department to fill out a paper form.

3. **Information Required**: You will need to provide:
   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.
   - The full names of the parents of the person named on the certificate.

4. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it can sometimes be issued on the same day with proof of the emergency.

6. **Contact Information**: If you need assistance, you can contact the Registration Department at:
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700 or +1 (246) 535-9751 (if calling from overseas)
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb

Make sure to complete your application in one go, as it cannot be saved to finish later.

Sources: [Get a copy of a birth certificate](https://alpha.gov.bb/family-birth-relationships/get-birth-certificate) · [Get a copy of a birth certificate](https://alpha.gov.bb/family-birth-relationships/get-birth-certificate/start)

*government-services · answered · 6.2s*

To apply for a Barbados driver's licence, follow these steps:

1. **Prepare Required Documents**:
   - Valid passport-sized photographs.
   - Proof of your age and residence.
   - Declaration of medical fitness.

2. **Complete the Application**:
   - Apply in the prescribed format to the Licensing Authority.

3. **Pass the Regulations Test**:
   - After document verification, you will need to take a Regulations Test. A handbook of traffic rules, signs, and regulations is provided with the application form.
   - If you pass, you will be issued a Learner's Permit.
   - If you fail, you can retake the test.

4. **Take the Driving Test**:
   - After obtaining the Learner's Permit, you will need to pass a driving test.

5. **Pay the Fees**:
   - Fees include:
     - Learner's Permit: $80.00
     - Regulation Test: $30.00
     - Driving Test: $100.00
     - Driver's License: $50.00
     - International Driver's License: $65.00 (if needed).

For more information, you can contact the Licensing Authority at:
- **Email**: blasupport@barbados.gov.bb
- **Phone**: 1-246-536-0264 / 1-246-536-0265.

Sources: [GOV.BB](https://www.gov.bb/Citizens/driver-licence)

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 7.8s*

The history of sugar in Barbados is pivotal to the island's development and transformation. Here are the key points:

- **Introduction of Sugar Cane**: Sugar cane was introduced to Barbados from Dutch Brazil around 1640. This marked a significant shift in the island's economy and society.
- **Shift from Tobacco to Sugar**: Initially, the early settlers cultivated tobacco and cotton, but poor economic returns led them to switch to sugar cane, which proved to be a more profitable cash crop despite its high capital and labor demands.
- **Plantation System**: The sugar industry required large investments and heavy labor, leading to the establishment of large plantations. Small farmers were gradually pushed out of production as the plantation system took over.
- **Labor Supply**: The industry relied heavily on African slaves, who were brought to work on the plantations. This drastically changed the demographic and cultural landscape of Barbados.
- **Economic Impact**: By the 18th century, Barbados became one of the world's largest sugar producers, with its economy heavily dependent on sugar exports. The estimated value of the colony in the early 1730s was around £5,500,000.
- **Cultural Influence**: The success of the sugar industry not only shaped the economy but also influenced the social structure and cultural identity of Barbados, intertwining it with the histories of slavery and colonialism.

Overall, the sugar industry was a driving force behind the economic and social changes in Barbados, establishing it as a key player in the Caribbean's sugar production.

## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 5.5s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an enslaved African who became a prominent figure in the uprising. The rebellion was fueled by the harsh conditions endured by enslaved people and the growing abolitionist movement in England, which inspired hopes for freedom among the enslaved population.

The planters were taken by surprise, believing their slaves were content due to certain allowances like weekly dances and social activities. However, the oppressive Barbados Slave Code, which denied slaves basic human rights and allowed for brutal punishments, contributed to the unrest.

Bussa and other leaders organized the revolt, aiming to negotiate for freedom, believing the political climate in Britain was favorable for such discussions. Unfortunately, the rebellion was swiftly suppressed by British forces, resulting in the deaths of hundreds of rebels. The brutality of the suppression shocked many in England and strengthened the abolitionist movement, ultimately leading to the abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833.

## 78. What is Harrison's Cave?
*visitors · answered · 4.5s*

Harrison's Cave is a popular tourist attraction located in the central uplands of Barbados. It features a stunning subterranean environment characterized by unique stalactite and stalagmite formations, which have been naturally created through water erosion of limestone rock. Visitors can explore the caves via a tramway, with opportunities to disembark and get closer to the formations at certain points during the tour. One of the notable areas within the cave is "The Great Hall," which has a height of over 50 feet (15 m). The cave was first mentioned in historical documents in 1795 and was rediscovered in the early 1970s, subsequently developed for public tours.

## 80. What should I know about driving in Barbados?
*visitors · answered · 5.8s*

- **Driving Side**: In Barbados, driving is on the left-hand side of the road.
- **Speed Limits**: The speed limit in built-up areas is 60 km/h (37 mph), while on the ABC Highway, it is generally 80 km/h (50 mph).
- **Road Conditions**: Barbados has 1,600 kilometers (990 miles) of public paved roads.
- **Vehicle Licensing**: To drive in Barbados, you must hold a valid driving license. If you are at least 18 years old, you can apply for a permanent license for a private motor vehicle. A Learner's Permit is required first.
- **International Driving Permit**: If you are a visitor, you may need an International Driving Permit (IDP) to drive legally in Barbados. This can be obtained in your home country before traveling.
- **Traffic Regulations**: Familiarize yourself with local traffic laws and regulations to ensure a safe driving experience.

For more detailed information about obtaining a driving license or an International Driving Permit, you can refer to the Barbados Licensing Authority.

## 84. What should I do to prepare for a hurricane?

- **Board up windows** if needed and bring in outdoor items.
- **Fill your vehicle with gas**.
- Ensure family members know your **emergency plan** and shelter arrangements.
- **Store at least 3 gallons of drinking water per person** for 3 days. Fill baths, buckets, and other containers before a storm.

- Stay informed by listening to local radio or watching TV news for updates and shelter information.
