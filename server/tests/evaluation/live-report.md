## 6. How do I renew my Barbados passport?
*resident-top-20 · answered · 5.4s*

To renew your Barbados passport, follow these steps:

1. **Book an appointment** online at the Immigration Department's website.
2. **Bring the following documents** to your appointment:
   - A completed Passport Form A (for those 16 and over) or Form B (for children).
   - Your previous passport.
   - Two passport-size photographs.
   - Your Barbados ID card (original and a copy).
3. **Pay the fee**:
   - BDS$150 for an adult.
   - BDS$100 for a child.
   - Payment is made when you apply.
4. **Additional notes**:
   - Form A and one photo must be certified by a guarantor.
   - Minors must be accompanied by a parent, and passports are collected by the holder.

For more details, you can visit the Immigration Department's website [here](https://immigration.gov.bb/pages/Passport.aspx).

## 14. How do I register a business in Barbados?
*resident-top-20 · answered · 4.8s*

To register a business in Barbados, you need to follow these steps:

- **Determine the Business Name**: Choose a name that complies with the Registration of Business Names Act.
- **File Application**: Submit your application for registration using Form 1. This can be done through the Business Barbados Portal.
- **Required Documents**: Include any necessary documents as annexes to your application.
- **Approval**: Business Barbados Officers will review your application. If everything is in order, your business will be registered under the approved name.

It's important to register your business name within fourteen (14) days after starting your business if it does not consist of your true surname or the true surnames of the partners involved. ## 21. How do I get a birth certificate in Barbados?
*government-services · answered · 7.9s*

To get a copy of a birth certificate in Barbados, you can follow these steps:

1. **Eligibility**: You can request a copy for anyone whose birth was registered in Barbados. If you are requesting on someone else's behalf, you must disclose your relationship to them and the reason for the request.

2. **Application Methods**: There are two ways to apply for a birth certificate:
   - **Online**: You will need a debit or credit card and an EZPay+ account. Complete the application form online.
   - **In-Person**: You can also complete a paper form and submit it at the Applications Desk.

3. **Information Required**: You will need to provide:
   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.
   - The full names of the parents of the person named on the certificate.

4. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it can sometimes be issued on the same day with proof of the emergency.

6. **Contact Information**: If you need further assistance or if you live overseas, you can contact the Registration Department at:
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700 or +1 (246) 535-9751 (if calling from overseas).

For more detailed information, you can visit the official government page on [getting a birth certificate](https://alpha.gov.bb/family-birth-relationships/get-birth-certificate).

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 6.3s*

The history of sugar in Barbados dates back to the 1640s when the island transitioned from a small farm economy, primarily growing tobacco and cotton, to large-scale sugar production. This shift was driven by the introduction of sugar cane as a cash crop, which proved to be labor and capital intensive. The small farmers were unable to sustain sugar production due to the financial demands, leading to the rise of large plantation owners.

The cultivation of sugar cane was significantly influenced by the exiled Jewish community, who introduced windmill technology for sugar processing. By the mid-17th century, Barbados had become home to over 500 windmills, making it the second highest in density worldwide, after the Netherlands.

The sugar industry not only transformed the economy but also led to the importation of enslaved Africans to work on the plantations, fundamentally altering the social and economic landscape of the island. This agricultural dominance continued until the 1990s when tourism began to eclipse sugar and rum as the primary economic driver for Barbados. The legacy of sugar production is preserved in various cultural and historical narratives, including the establishment of the Sir Frank Hutson Sugar Museum, which showcases the history and machinery of sugar production in the 18th and 19th centuries.

## 54. What is the Barbados Garrison?
*history-culture · answered · 5.1s*

The Barbados Garrison is a historic military complex that was the largest in the British Colonies during the 18th and 19th centuries. It began with the construction of St. Ann's Fort in 1705 and expanded to include soldiers' barracks, a parade ground, and a commissariat. The Garrison played a crucial role in the defense of the island and reflects the military history of Barbados, particularly during the colonial period when various European powers vied for control over the Caribbean. Today, it is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site known as Historic Bridgetown and Its Garrison, which was inscribed in 2011 and includes numerous landmark buildings from the 17th to 19th centuries.

## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 5.3s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an African-born slave, and was fueled by the intolerable conditions faced by enslaved individuals on the island. Despite the planters believing that their slaves were content due to allowances for social activities, the harsh realities of the Barbados Slave Code, which denied slaves basic human rights, contributed to the uprising.

The rebellion was characterized by a growing sentiment among the enslaved population that their treatment was unacceptable, and many believed that the political climate in Britain was favorable for negotiating freedom. Bussa emerged as a prominent figure among the organizers, alongside others, including a woman named Nanny Grigg.

Unfortunately, the rebellion was poorly timed and ultimately failed, as the enslaved rebels were outmatched by British forces. The uprising was suppressed quickly, resulting in the deaths of hundreds of rebels. The aftermath of the rebellion shocked the British public and strengthened the abolitionist movement, leading to the eventual abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833. The rebellion highlighted the deep-seated issues of slavery and the desire for freedom among the enslaved population in Barbados.

- **Performance Context**: It is often associated with local festivals and celebrations, showcasing the vibrant musical heritage of Barbados.

## 91. What exactly are Bajan fish cakes, and how are they prepared?
*client · answered · 6.4s*

Bajan fish cakes are a traditional dish in Barbados, typically made from salted codfish mixed with flour, spices, and seasonings. They are often enjoyed as a breakfast item or as a snack, and can be served on their own or in a sandwich known as "bread and two" (fish cakes in salt bread). 

### Ingredients:
- 1 lb boneless, skinless salt cod
- 1 cup flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 small hot pepper, finely diced
- Chopped parsley, thyme, and marjoram
- 1 egg, lightly beaten
- 1 cup water

### Preparation Steps:
1. **Prepare the Salted Cod:** Boil the salt cod in 6 cups of water for 4 minutes, then discard the water. Boil it again in fresh water for 3 minutes. Flake the fish with a fork after boiling.
2. **Mix Ingredients:** In a bowl, combine the flaked fish with the flour, baking powder, onion, hot pepper, herbs, and the beaten egg. Gradually add water until a thick batter forms.
3. **Fry the Fish Cakes:** Drop spoonfuls of the batter into hot oil over medium heat, being careful not to overcrowd the pan. Fry until golden brown, turning as necessary to ensure even cooking.
4. **Drain and Serve:** Remove the fish cakes from the oil and drain on paper towels. They are best enjoyed hot, often with a dipping sauce like Marie Rose sauce or traditional Barbados pepper sauce.

## 92. What is the difference between pudding and souse and cou-cou and flying fish?
*client · answered · 5.6s*

**Pudding and Souse**  
- **Pudding**: Made from grated sweet potato seasoned with onion, thyme, chives, a little sugar, salt, and butter, then cooked (traditionally baked).  
- **Souse**: Consists of boiled pork (often using the head, tails, and feet) pickled in lime juice, mixed with grated cucumber, sweet peppers, onion, parsley, and scotch bonnet pepper.  
- **Serving**: Both components are served together, and this dish is traditionally enjoyed on Saturdays in Barbados.

**Cou-Cou and Flying Fish**  
- **Cou-Cou**: A dish made primarily from cornmeal and okra, often served as a side. It is a staple in Barbadian cuisine and can be paired with various meats and fish.
- **Flying Fish**: A fish that is a national symbol of Barbados and is often prepared in various ways, including fried or baked. It is commonly served alongside cou-cou, making it a traditional meal known as "Cou-Cou and Flying Fish."

In summary, pudding and souse is a dish featuring pork and sweet potato, while cou-cou and flying fish is a combination of a cornmeal dish and a fish, both integral to Barbadian culinary traditions.
