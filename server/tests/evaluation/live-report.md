## 6. How do I renew my Barbados passport?
*resident-top-20 · answered · 6.4s*

To renew your Barbados passport, follow these steps:

1. **Book an Appointment**: Schedule an appointment online on the Immigration Department website.
2. **Prepare Required Documents**:
   - A completed Passport Form A (for those 16 and over) or Form B (for children).
   - Your previous passport.
   - Two passport-size photographs.
   - Your Barbados ID card (original and a copy).
3. **Pay the Fee**: The fee is BDS$150 for an adult and BDS$100 for a child, payable at the time of application.
4. **Guarantor Certification**: Form A and one photo must be certified by a guarantor.
5. **Minors**: If renewing for a minor, they must come with a parent, and passports are collected by the holder.

For more details, visit the [Immigration Department's website](https://immigration.gov.bb/pages/Passport.aspx).

   - Your name, address, and Barbados National Identification (ID) number.
   - The full name, date of birth, place of birth, and place of baptism of the person named on the certificate.

4. **Cost**: Each certified copy costs $5 BBD. For individuals aged 60 and over, the cost is $1 BBD per certificate.

5. **Processing Time**: A certificate is usually ready for collection in 5 to 7 business days. In cases of medical or travel emergencies, it can sometimes be issued on the same day with proof of the emergency.

6. **Contact Information**: If you need further assistance or wish to apply from overseas, you can contact the Registration Department at:
   - Email: registrarsupremecourt@barbados.gov.bb
   - Phone: (246) 535-9700 or +1 (246) 535-9751 (if calling from overseas).

## 52. What is the history of sugar in Barbados?
*history-culture · answered · 5.9s*

The history of sugar in Barbados dates back to the early 17th century. Initially, after the English settled in 1625, the island's economy was based on small-scale farming of tobacco and cotton. However, poor economic returns from these crops led to a shift towards sugar cane cultivation in the 1640s, which became the dominant agricultural product.

- **Transition to Sugar**: The introduction of sugar cane was a significant turning point, as it required large-scale production and labor. This led to the consolidation of land into large estates and the decline of small farmers.
- **Labor Source**: The sugar industry relied heavily on enslaved African labor, which transformed the demographic and economic landscape of Barbados. By the late 17th century, the island had become a major sugar producer in the Caribbean, with over 500 windmills for sugar processing.
- **Economic Impact**: The wealth generated from sugar production significantly influenced Barbados's economy and society, making it a key player in the transatlantic trade.
- **Decline of Sugar**: By the 1990s, the sugar and rum industry began to decline as tourism became the primary economic driver for the island.

Overall, sugar cultivation and production have played a crucial role in shaping Barbados's history, economy, and culture, with lasting impacts that are still evident today.

## 54. What is the Barbados Garrison?
*history-culture · answered · 4.3s*

The Barbados Garrison is a historic military complex that was the largest in the British Colonies during the 18th and 19th centuries. It began with the construction of St. Ann's Fort in 1705 and expanded to include soldiers' barracks, a parade ground, and a commissariat. The Garrison played a crucial role in protecting the island during conflicts among colonial powers such as England, France, Spain, and Holland. Today, it is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site known as Historic Bridgetown and Its Garrison, which was inscribed in 2011 and features a blend of historic buildings and modern amenities.

## 56. What is the story of Bussa's rebellion?
*history-culture · answered · 5.4s*

Bussa's Rebellion, which occurred in 1816, was the largest slave revolt in Barbadian history. It was led by Bussa, an African-born slave, and was fueled by the intolerable conditions faced by enslaved individuals under the Barbados Slave Code, which denied them basic human rights and subjected them to severe punishments.

The rebellion was surprising to the planters, who believed their slaves were content due to allowances for social activities and basic care. However, a growing sentiment among the enslaved population indicated that they were ready to fight for their freedom, especially as the political climate in Britain seemed favorable for negotiations.

Bussa became a prominent figure among the rebellion's organizers, which included other enslaved persons and literate freedmen. Despite their efforts, the uprising was poorly timed and ultimately failed, with British forces quickly suppressing the revolt and resulting in the deaths of many rebels. The aftermath of the rebellion shocked the British public and contributed to the growing abolitionist movement, leading to the eventual abolition of slavery in the British Empire in 1833.

## 58. What is tuk band music?
*history-culture · answered · 5.2s*

Tuk band music, also known as rukatuk, is a traditional Barbadian musical genre that emerged during the colonial era. It combines elements of African-based rhythms with European military music, reflecting the cultural fusion that occurred in Barbados. The music is typically performed by a tuk band, which consists of instruments such as a double-headed bass drum, triangle, flute, snare drum, and the pennywhistle, which has replaced the traditional fiddle.

The genre is symbolic of Barbadian cultural history and has roots in the colonial period when traditional African drums were banned. Tuk band music is characterized by its lively and rhythmic sound, often associated with local celebrations and cultural events. Although it is not as popular as it once was, it remains an important part of Barbados's musical heritage.

## 78. What is Harrison's Cave?

Harrison's Cave is a popular tourist attraction located in the central uplands of Barbados. It features a stunning subterranean environment formed by water erosion through limestone rock, resulting in unique stalactite and stalagmite formations. It is recognized as one of the "Seven Wonders of Barbados" and offers various tours, including a tram tour and an eco-adventure tour for those seeking a more hands-on experience.

## 91. What exactly are Bajan fish cakes, and how are they prepared?
*client · answered · 7.6s*

Bajan fish cakes are a traditional dish in Barbados, typically made from salted codfish mixed with flour, spices, and seasonings, then deep-fried until golden brown. They are often enjoyed as a snack or part of a meal, sometimes served in a sandwich known as "bread and two" with salt bread.

### Ingredients:
- 1 lb boneless, skinless salt cod
- 1 cup flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 small hot pepper, finely diced
- Chopped parsley, thyme, and marjoram
- 1 cup water
- 1 egg, lightly beaten

### Preparation Steps:
1. **Prepare the Salted Cod:** Boil the salt cod in 6 cups of water for 4 minutes, then discard the water. Boil again in fresh water for 3 minutes. Flake the fish with a fork after draining.
2. **Mix Ingredients:** In a bowl, combine the flaked fish with the flour, baking powder, onion, hot pepper, herbs, and egg. Gradually add water until a thick batter forms.
3. **Fry the Cakes:** Drop spoonfuls of the batter into hot oil over medium heat, being careful not to overcrowd the pan. Fry until golden brown, adjusting the heat as necessary to ensure they cook through without burning.
4. **Drain and Serve:** Drain on paper towels and serve hot, often with a dipping sauce like Marie Rose sauce or traditional Barbados pepper sauce.

Bajan fish cakes are beloved for their crispy exterior and flavorful interior, making them a popular choice among locals and visitors alike.

Sources: [Barbados Recipes: Fish Cakes](https://barbados.org/barbados-recipes-fishcakes.htm) · [Fish Cakes and Sauce Marie Rose Recipe - Totally Barbados](https://www.totallybarbados.com/articles/barbados-recipes/fish-cakes-sauce-marie-rose-recipe) · [Explore Barbados Street Food](https://www.visitbarbados.org/barbados-street-food)

## 92. What is the difference between pudding and souse and cou-cou and flying fish?
*client · answered · 6.9s*

**Pudding and Souse**  
- **Pudding**: Made from grated sweet potato seasoned with onion, thyme, chives, a little sugar, salt, and butter, then cooked (usually baked).  
- **Souse**: Consists of boiled pork (often from the head, tails, and feet) pickled in lime juice with grated cucumber, sweet peppers, onion, parsley, and scotch bonnet pepper.  
- **Serving**: These two components are served together and are a traditional dish enjoyed island-wide on Saturdays.

**Cou-Cou and Flying Fish**  
- **Cou-Cou**: A dish made primarily from cornmeal and okra, often served as a side. It is a staple in Barbadian cuisine and can accompany various meats and fish.
- **Flying Fish**: A fish that is a national symbol of Barbados and is often prepared in various ways, including fried or baked. It is commonly served with cou-cou, making it a traditional meal known as "Cou-Cou and Flying Fish."

In summary, pudding and souse is a dish featuring sweet potato and pickled pork, while cou-cou and flying fish is a combination of cornmeal-based cou-cou served with the fish, highlighting different aspects of Barbadian culinary traditions.
